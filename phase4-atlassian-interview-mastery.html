Know the source

Interview Process Overview

Processes change by role, level, and location. Confirm every loop with your recruiter. This section separates official public material from practice assumptions.

Official source. Atlassian publishes an engineering interview handbook: atlassian.com/company/careers/resources/interviewing/engineering. Values: atlassian.com/company/values. Careers hub: atlassian.com/company/careers. Re-read those pages before your loop. This file is not a substitute for recruiter email.

What Atlassian says publicly (official)

Summarized from the public engineering handbook (as of this file’s writing). Not a guarantee of your loop.

  • Coding. They describe assessing problem-solving and learning agility, not language trivia. You can typically choose a language. They mention a coding stage with Data Structures and Code Design parts. Clean thinking and trade-offs matter more than a missing line.
  • System design (~60 min in the handbook). Not “write production code.” They want how you explore a problem: questions, reliability, cost, who you’d partner with, technologies you’d use. Problems ladder — follow-ups get harder or easier. Multiple viable answers.
  • Manager interview. Usually hiring manager or a more senior manager. Who you are, what you want, how you collaborate, past projects including why the work existed (business justification).
  • Values interview. Alignment with the five published values. Values ≠ office culture. Interviewer is often not on the hiring team (could be another function). Informal-feeling, still evaluative. Official design guide also mentions STAR and ~45 minutes for values — confirm your invite.
  • Hiring Committee. After interviews, feedback is consolidated. A committee reviews independently (feedback + CV) for a consistent decision.

They also state they do not hire against one perfect candidate profile, and they invite accommodation requests via the talent partner.

Practice assumptions (not official)

Practice assumption. Many senior loops look like: recruiter screen → one or more coding / craft rounds → system design → hiring manager → values → committee. Your invite may differ (take-home, extra design, no second coding, India vs other regions). Do not argue with a recruiter using this paragraph.

Opening

Tell Me About Yourself

Structure: Present → Experience → Strongest areas → Impact → Current scope → What I want → Why Atlassian (one clause). Not a chronological CV. Not your childhood.

Timed versions — fill with YOUR facts

Avoid: reciting every job; diving into Fiber; “passionate team player”; long personal origin stories unless asked.

Example 30s (structure only — replace facts): “I’m a senior engineer with seven years on product UIs and the Node APIs behind them. Recently I’ve owned [mention one surface]. I’m looking for a place where collaboration software is the product — that’s why I’m talking to Atlassian.”

Example 60s: Add one concrete impact and one technical strength (e.g. performance or architecture). One sentence on current scope (what you decide vs escalate).

Example 90s: Add a second domain (mentoring or cross-team) and a honest “why now.”

Example 2 min: Only if they lean in. One project arc: problem → decision → result → lesson. Then stop and ask what they want to go deep on.

Behavioral Question Bank

Practice behavioral questions for senior React/TypeScript/Node loops — including values and hiring-manager conversations. Not official or leaked Atlassian questions. Answer aloud using your Story Bank; frameworks here are guides, not scripts. No invented experiences.

LeadershipQ1

1. Tell me about a time you led a cross-functional initiative without being the formal tech lead.

What interviewer is testing. Whether you create direction and alignment when the org chart is silent — not whether you had a title.

Weak answer looks like. “I was the senior so people listened.” No named stakeholders, no decision record, hero narrative.

Strong answer should contain. STAR-L: Situation (multi-team gap) → Task (outcome you owned) → Actions in first person (who you aligned, what you wrote, how you decided) → Result with honest metrics → Learning. Name PM/design/backend counterparts. One explicit trade-off you drove.

Follow-up questions. What would you do if the PM disagreed? How did you know you were succeeding before launch?

Suggested story categories. Leadership, Cross-team collaboration, Influencing without authority, Most impactful project

LeadershipQ2

2. Describe when you set technical direction during uncertainty.

What interviewer is testing. Judgment under incomplete information — can you commit, communicate reversibility, and bring others along?

Weak answer looks like. Listing technologies without context; “we picked React because I like it”; no alternatives rejected.

Strong answer should contain. Context → two viable options → decision criteria (customer harm, reversibility, team skill, 10× cost) → chosen path → who you socialized with → kill criterion if wrong. Mention RFC/ADR or equivalent even if informal.

Follow-up questions. What signal would make you reverse the decision? Who did you not include and why?

Suggested story categories. Architecture decision, Ambiguous requirement, Technical debt, Leadership

LeadershipQ3

3. Tell me about rallying engineers around an unpopular but necessary decision.

What interviewer is testing. Influence through reasoning and empathy, not rank — especially relevant to senior IC loops.

Weak answer looks like. Framing dissenters as obstacles; “they just didn’t get it”; no acknowledgment of their reasonable concern.

Strong answer should contain. Name the unpopular decision and why it hurt locally (velocity, pride, tech preference). Your Actions: listened first, reframed in customer/outage terms, offered mitigation (flags, timeline, scope). Result: what shipped or what risk dropped. Learning: what you’d communicate earlier next time.

Follow-up questions. Did anyone still disagree at ship? Would you make the same call?

Suggested story categories. Disagreement, Influencing without authority, Technical debt, Leadership

LeadershipQ4

4. When did you step up as de facto lead during reorg or attrition?

What interviewer is testing. Stability and continuity when leadership vacuum appears — without empire-building.

Weak answer looks like. Complaining about chaos; only describing your tickets; claiming you “saved everything.”

Strong answer should contain. Situation: what broke (bus factor, on-call, roadmap). Task: what outcome the team needed. Actions: rituals you started (standups, RFCs, pairing), how you distributed load, how you escalated what wasn’t yours. Result: team throughput or incident trend. Learning: what you handed back when a manager arrived.

Follow-up questions. How did you avoid burnout? What did you stop doing?

Suggested story categories. Leadership, Production outage, Cross-team collaboration, Mentoring

LeadershipQ5

5. Describe leading a migration others were afraid to touch.

What interviewer is testing. Courage plus plan — phased rollout, measurable risk reduction, not big-bang bravado.

Weak answer looks like. “It wasn’t that hard”; blaming the previous team; no rollback story.

Strong answer should contain. Why the migration mattered (perf, security, hiring). Phases: read path, dual-write, cutover, delete. Your slice: code, comms, training. Metrics: error rate, latency, tickets. Trade-off: speed vs safety. Flag/feature-toggle language for React/Node context.

Follow-up questions. What would have aborted the migration? How long did you run dual-write?

Suggested story categories. Architecture decision, Technical debt, Performance improvement, Most impactful project

Concurrent meeting peak

Given an array of meetings `[start, end)` as half-open integer minutes, return the maximum number of meetings happening at the same time. Meetings touching at an endpoint do not overlap (e.g. [0,5) and [5,10) → peak 1).

Clarify first. Are inputs sorted? Can end ≤ start? Empty input? Integer overflow on count?

Sort by start time. Sweep line: +1 at start, −1 at end. Track running max.

TypeScript
function maxConcurrent(meetings: [number, number][]): number {
  if (meetings.length === 0) return 0;
  const events: [number, number][] = [];
  for (const [s, e] of meetings) {
    if (e <= s) continue;
    events.push([s, 1], [e, -1]);
  }
  events.sort((a, b) => a[0] - b[0] || a[1] - b[1]);
  let cur = 0, best = 0;
  for (const [, d] of events) {
    cur += d;
    if (cur > best) best = cur;
  }
  return best;
}

Complexity. O(n log n) time for sort, O(n) space for events.

Edge cases. Empty; single meeting; all nested; all disjoint; duplicate intervals; end equals start.

Communication checklist. State half-open convention. Mention brute force pairwise O(n²). Offer sweep after sort.

Versioned config merge

You receive config layers as objects (string → unknown). Later layers override earlier keys. If a value is the sentinel `{ "__delete__": true }`, remove that key from the merged result (even if a prior layer set it). Return the merged flat object.

Clarify first. Deep merge or flat only? Nested objects? Key types? Mutate inputs?

Single pass over layers in order. Track current map. Deletes remove keys outright.

TypeScript
const DELETE = { __delete__: true } as const;

function isDelete(v: unknown): v is typeof DELETE {
  return !!v && typeof v === "object" && (v as any).__delete__ === true;
}

function mergeConfigs(layers: Record<string, unknown>[]): Record<string, unknown> {
  const out: Record<string, unknown> = {};
  for (const layer of layers) {
    for (const [k, v] of Object.entries(layer)) {
      if (isDelete(v)) delete out[k];
      else out[k] = v;
    }
  }
  return out;
}

Complexity. O(total keys across layers) time and space.

Edge cases. No layers; delete never set; delete then re-set in later layer; empty strings as keys.

Communication checklist. Clarify flat vs deep merge early. Mention production uses schema validation after merge.

Sliding window request counts

Implement `RateWindow` with `record(userId, tsMs)` and `count(userId, nowMs, windowMs)` returning how many events from that user fall in `(nowMs - windowMs, nowMs]`. Assume timestamps are non-decreasing within a single user's calls.

Clarify first. Per-user isolation? Memory bound? Thread-safe? Exact vs approximate?

Deque of timestamps per user. Drop from front while ts ≤ now − window.

TypeScript
class RateWindow {
  private buckets = new Map<string, number[]>();

  record(userId: string, tsMs: number): void {
    const q = this.buckets.get(userId) ?? [];
    q.push(tsMs);
    this.buckets.set(userId, q);
  }

  count(userId: string, nowMs: number, windowMs: number): number {
    const q = this.buckets.get(userId);
    if (!q) return 0;
    const cutoff = nowMs - windowMs;
    while (q.length && q[0] <= cutoff) q.shift();
    return q.length;
  }
}

Complexity. Amortized O(1) per record; count may evict stale in O(k). Space O(events retained).

Edge cases. WindowMs = 0; first event; burst at boundary; unknown user returns 0.

Communication checklist. Compare fixed window vs sliding. Mention Redis ZSET for distributed version.

Comment thread flatten with depth

Given nested comments `{ id, body, replies[] }`, return a flat array of `{ id, body, depth }` in pre-order (parent before children). `depth` starts at 0 for roots.

Clarify first. Max depth? Cycle detection? Stable order among siblings? Empty replies?

DFS. Pass depth+1 to children. Iterative stack if recursion depth is a concern.

TypeScript
type Comment = { id: string; body: string; replies?: Comment[] };
type Flat = { id: string; body: string; depth: number };

function flattenComments(roots: Comment[]): Flat[] {
  const out: Flat[] = [];
  const walk = (nodes: Comment[], depth: number) => {
    for (const n of nodes) {
      out.push({ id: n.id, body: n.body, depth });
      if (n.replies?.length) walk(n.replies, depth + 1);
    }
  };
  walk(roots, 0);
  return out;
}

Complexity. O(n) time and output space for n nodes.

Edge cases. Empty forest; single node; very deep thread; missing replies field.

Communication checklist. Offer iterative version if stack overflow is mentioned. Discuss pagination for UI.

Top K movers in a stream

Process a stream of `(userId, delta)` score updates. After each update, be ready to return the top K userIds by total score (ties broken by lexicographic userId ascending). Implement `update(userId, delta)` and `topK()`.

Clarify first. Can scores go negative? K vs number of users? Return fewer if < K users?

HashMap totals. For topK, sort entries O(u log u) or min-heap of size K when K ≪ u.

TypeScript
class TopKMovers {
  private scores = new Map<string, number>();

  update(userId: string, delta: number): void {
    this.scores.set(userId, (this.scores.get(userId) ?? 0) + delta);
  }

  topK(k: number): string[] {
    return [...this.scores.entries()]
      .sort((a, b) => b[1] - a[1] || a[0].localeCompare(b[0]))
      .slice(0, k)
      .map(([id]) => id);
  }
}

Complexity. Update O(1). topK sort O(u log u); heap variant O(u log k).

Edge cases. K = 0; negative deltas; tie on score; update same user many times.

Communication checklist. Ask if topK is hot path. Mention heap + lazy deletion for production leaderboard.

Path normalization checker

Given a Unix-style path string (segments separated by `/`, may include `.` and `..`), return the normalized absolute path starting with `/`, or `""` if the path escapes above root (more `..` than prior segments allow). No trailing slash except root `/`.

Clarify first. Windows paths? Multiple slashes? Empty string? Relative vs absolute input?

Split on `/`, filter empty and `.`, stack push/pop on `..`.

TypeScript
function normalizePath(path: string): string {
  const stack: string[] = [];
  for (const part of path.split("/")) {
    if (!part || part === ".") continue;
    if (part === "..") {
      if (stack.length === 0) return "";
      stack.pop();
    } else stack.push(part);
  }
  return "/" + stack.join("/");
}

Complexity. O(n) time and space for n characters.

Edge cases. Already normalized; only dots; deep nesting; root path `/`.

Communication checklist. Relate to browser/router path handling. Mention symlink semantics out of scope.

Bijection pattern match

Given a pattern of uppercase letters `A-Z` and a space-separated sentence, return whether each letter maps bijectively to exactly one word (same length as pattern). Same letter → same word; different letters → different words.

Clarify first. Case sensitivity? Extra spaces? Pattern length must match word count?

Two maps: char→word and word→char. On conflict return false.

TypeScript
function matchesPattern(pattern: string, sentence: string): boolean {
  const words = sentence.trim().split(/\s+/);
  if (words.length !== pattern.length) return false;
  const c2w = new Map<string, string>();
  const w2c = new Map<string, string>();
  for (let i = 0; i < pattern.length; i++) {
    const c = pattern[i], w = words[i];
    const prevW = c2w.get(c);
    const prevC = w2c.get(w);
    if (prevW !== undefined && prevW !== w) return false;
    if (prevC !== undefined && prevC !== c) return false;
    c2w.set(c, w);
    w2c.set(w, c);
  }
  return true;
}

Complexity. O(L + W) time; O(unique letters + words) space.

Edge cases. Length mismatch; repeated words with different letters; single char pattern.

Communication checklist. Connect to permission key aliasing or URL slug bijection in product code.

Task cooldown scheduler

You have tasks labeled `A-Z` in an array `tasks` and a cooldown `n` (minimum idle slots between two identical tasks). One slot executes one task or idle. Return the minimum number of slots to finish all tasks.

Clarify first. Only one CPU? Tasks all length 1? What if n = 0?

Count frequencies. Greedily place most frequent with gaps. Formula: max(len, (maxFreq-1)*(n+1)+countMax).

TypeScript
function leastInterval(tasks: string[], n: number): number {
  const freq = new Map<string, number>();
  for (const t of tasks) freq.set(t, (freq.get(t) ?? 0) + 1);
  let maxF = 0, maxCount = 0;
  for (const f of freq.values()) {
    if (f > maxF) { maxF = f; maxCount = 1; }
    else if (f === maxF) maxCount++;
  }
  const part = (maxF - 1) * (n + 1) + maxCount;
  return Math.max(tasks.length, part);
}

Complexity. O(t) to count, O(1) extra for 26 letters.

Edge cases. Single task type; n larger than task count; all unique tasks.

Communication checklist. Walk small example on whiteboard. Mention heap simulation if they want explicit schedule.

Atlassian senior loops grade reasoning, not buzzwords. Specificity beats volume. Structure beats speed. These patterns apply to coding, design, and behavioral answers.

Thinking aloud without noise

  1. Signpost. "I'll clarify, then brute force, then optimize." Buys time and shows structure.
  2. Pause with purpose. "Give me 45 seconds to write the loop, then I'll walk it." Silence without context reads as stuck.
  3. Name the invariant. One sentence: "The window always contains at most K distinct chars."
  4. Challenge assumptions politely. "Can we mutate the input? If not, I'll copy first."
  5. Close with trade-offs. "This is O(n) space; if memory were tight I'd sort and two-pointer at O(n log n)."

  • Consistency: stale reads OK? Exactly-once needed?
  • Failure: fail open or closed? Partial success?
  • Scope: MVP in 45 minutes vs production system?

Senior engineers say "I don't know" with a plan. Bluffing destroys values interviews. These frameworks keep you in the conversation.

Reason from the model

  1. State what you do know (adjacent system, constraint, user need).
  2. Name the unknown explicitly.
  3. Derive a reasonable approach from first principles or analogous system.
  4. Say what you'd validate first (metric, prototype, doc).
  5. Invite correction: "Does that match how you use it here?"

Execution hygiene

Interview Day Strategy

24 hours before

  • Light revision only — one story, one SD outline, one coding pattern. No new topics.
  • Confirm schedule, timezone, links, and interviewers if shared.
  • Prepare physical space: camera height, light, quiet, water, charger.
  • Clothes ready; reduce morning decisions.
  • Sleep target 7+ hours — cognitive performance beats one extra hour of cramming.

2 hours before

  • Eat lightly; caffeine if you use it — not more than usual.
  • Open this file: Story Bank titles, TMAY draft, Tech Checklist.
  • Test mic/camera and screen share if required.
  • Close noisy apps; phone on silent; notify household.

30 minutes before

  • Bathroom, water, posture check.
  • Two-minute TMAY out loud — stop when done, do not rewrite.
  • One calm breath routine: inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 6 — twice.
  • Join link 2–3 minutes early, not 15 (avoid awkward idle chat unless culture expects it).

During the interview

  • Clarify before solving. Write assumptions.
  • Narrate at decision points; go quiet only when you said you would code.
  • Whiteboard / editor: leave margin, label boxes, date the diagram if multiple versions.
  • Hints are gifts — acknowledge and adjust.
  • Leave 3–5 minutes for your questions.

Between rounds (same day)

  • Stand, walk, water — do not replay mistakes aloud for 20 minutes.
  • One sentence debrief: what to adjust next round (pace, clarify, story cut).
  • Different story for behavioral if overlap risk with earlier interviewer notes.

After the interview

  • Within 24h: brief thank-you to recruiter or coordinator if appropriate — specific, short.
  • Log debrief in Rejection Analysis (even if it went well — capture what to repeat).
  • No post-mortem spiral; one corrective action for next mock.
